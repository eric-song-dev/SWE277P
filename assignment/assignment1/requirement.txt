3. Team Ground Rules
Establishing a written set of team ground rules is a good way to promote a high-functioning, productive, and successful team. Think of this activity as "designing the perfect team member." You can refer to these rules as something objective that you have all agreed to when you go through some of the more difficult stages of team formation (i.e., storming). These rules should touch on things like:

values about work quality
processes for reviewing each other's work
norms that regulate how and when members are expected to interact/communicate with each other
rules for communication (e.g., no interrupting, etc. See the slide about "effective interpersonal behaviors" from Lecture 1)
processes for informing others of unanticipated events that adversely affect your attendance or work, and how to compensate for those
anything else you can think of that would be helpful. Think again of your best/worst team experiences and what rules you could put in place to ensure more of the best and less of the worst! Here are some suggestions: When someone emails or texts, how soon is a response expected? How do people feel about starting meeting strictly on-time versus having a more laissez faire attitude? Will groups meet even if not ever member can be present? If someone isn't present, who is responsible for filling them in? What are the responsibilities for the group member who was absent?
**Include at least 10 ground rules.
